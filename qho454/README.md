# QHO454 — Beginning as a Researcher

This folder maps the existing psychology research-methods portfolio to the released QHO454 Portfolio 1 materials and, crucially, to the marking behaviours rewarded in the issued brief, checklist, FAQs and formative feedback.

## Released assessment structure

Portfolio 1 contains two tasks:

1. **Methods section critique** — 500 words
2. **Describing and displaying data** — 500 words

Each task contributes 50% of the portfolio mark.

## Learning outcomes

The released brief identifies five learning outcomes:

- **LO1** — Explain the use of varying quantitative and qualitative approaches to the study of Psychology.
- **LO2** — Identify and apply a variety of quantitative and qualitative research methods and analytical techniques in differing real-world psychological contexts.
- **LO3** — Consider the ethical implications of different study designs and conduct research under close supervision.
- **LO4** — Identify and reflect on values central to practice in Psychology and respond in an ethical manner in a variety of contexts.
- **LO5** — Apply findings from quantitative and qualitative psychological research to the study of personal, social, community and organisational issues.

## Marking-criteria target

This directory is not satisfied by broad topic coverage alone. Evidence is mapped to the concrete behaviours rewarded by the marking criteria.

### Task 1 — high-mark beats

Evidence should demonstrate that the critique:

- identifies the major deficiencies across **Design, Participants, Materials, Procedure and Ethical Considerations**;
- shows judgement about which issues are important;
- evaluates why each problem matters for validity, clarity, quality, ethics or replicability;
- gives a specific correction for each material deficiency;
- uses corrections that are methodologically accurate and convincing;
- is clearly organised and succinct.

### Task 2 — high-mark beats

Evidence should demonstrate that:

- summary statistics are informative, appropriate, relevant and accurate;
- interpretation shows insight into the actual data rather than simply repeating output;
- reporting conventions are applied consistently;
- each graph/table is appropriate, accurate, clearly labelled and cleanly formatted;
- missing values and unequal group sizes are handled sensibly;
- descriptive comparisons are not overstated as causal effects.
