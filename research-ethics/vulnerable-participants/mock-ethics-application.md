# Mock Ethics Application: Research with Vulnerable Participants

> **Portfolio status:** Simulated research-governance exercise. This is not evidence of formal ethical approval and no real participants were recruited.

## Proposed study

**Title:** Social Isolation, Daily Routine and Psychological Wellbeing in Adults Receiving Community Mental-Health Support

**Aim:** To examine associations between perceived social isolation, daily routine stability and self-reported psychological wellbeing.

**Design:** Cross-sectional questionnaire study using brief validated-style self-report measures and demographic questions.

**Population:** Adults aged 18+ receiving community mental-health support.

## Why this population requires additional protection

Participants may experience fluctuating distress, reduced confidence in declining requests from professionals, or concerns that participation could affect care. The study therefore requires enhanced safeguards around voluntariness, capacity, privacy, distress and safeguarding.

## Recruitment safeguards

- Recruitment material must state clearly that participation is optional.
- Treating clinicians should not pressure individuals to participate.
- Declining or withdrawing must have no effect on treatment, benefits, services or relationships with staff.
- Recruitment language should avoid therapeutic claims.
- No disproportionate incentive should be used.

## Consent and capacity

Consent is treated as an ongoing process rather than a single signature.

Before participation, the researcher checks that the participant can:
1. understand the purpose and procedures;
2. retain enough information to decide;
3. weigh foreseeable risks and benefits;
4. communicate a voluntary decision.

If these conditions are not met, the person will not be enrolled in this simulated protocol.

## Foreseeable risks

| Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Emotional discomfort when discussing isolation | Moderate | Low–moderate | Skip items, pause, withdraw, debrief |
| Perceived pressure to participate | Low–moderate | Moderate | Independent wording, explicit voluntariness |
| Confidentiality breach | Low | Moderate–high | Data minimisation, pseudonymisation, restricted access |
| Disclosure indicating immediate risk | Low | High | Predefined safeguarding escalation procedure |

## Withdrawal

Participants may stop at any time without giving a reason. They may request deletion of identifiable data until the point at which data have been irreversibly anonymised and aggregated.

## Safeguarding

If a participant discloses an immediate and credible risk of serious harm to themselves or another person, confidentiality may need to be limited in accordance with the information given before consent. The researcher would escalate only the minimum necessary information through the approved safeguarding pathway.

## Data handling

- Collect the minimum data necessary.
- Use participant IDs rather than names in analysis files.
- Store contact information separately from research responses.
- Restrict access to authorised research personnel.
- Define retention and deletion periods in advance.
- Report only aggregate or appropriately anonymised findings.

## Debrief

Participants receive a plain-language explanation of the study, reminder of withdrawal rights, researcher contact information, and appropriate support/signposting information.

## Ethical judgement

**Decision:** Suitable to proceed only after safeguards are in place.

The central ethical challenge is not whether the population can participate in research, but whether the design protects autonomy, reduces unequal power, anticipates distress and ensures that participation remains genuinely voluntary.
