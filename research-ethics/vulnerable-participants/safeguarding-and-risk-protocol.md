# Safeguarding and Risk Protocol

> Simulated research-governance exercise.

## Purpose

This protocol demonstrates how a researcher should respond to distress or safeguarding concerns while preserving participant autonomy and confidentiality as far as possible.

## Distress procedure

1. Notice signs of discomfort or a participant request to stop.
2. Pause data collection immediately.
3. Ask whether the participant wants to continue, skip the topic, take a break or withdraw.
4. Do not pressure the participant to explain.
5. If appropriate, provide the pre-prepared support/signposting sheet.
6. Record only information necessary for the research or safeguarding record.

## Risk disclosure decision pathway

### A. General distress, no indication of immediate danger
- Respect confidentiality.
- Offer to stop participation.
- Provide support information.
- Do not attempt to act as the participant's clinician.

### B. Concerning disclosure but no immediate or specific threat
- Clarify only what is necessary to understand whether safeguarding action is required.
- Follow the institution's approved escalation pathway.
- Consult the designated supervisor/safeguarding lead where permitted.

### C. Immediate and credible risk of serious harm
- Explain the need to act if it is safe to do so.
- Share only the minimum necessary information.
- Contact the designated safeguarding or emergency route specified in the approved protocol.
- Document the decision and rationale.

## Ethical principles demonstrated

- Respect for autonomy
- Proportionality
- Data minimisation
- Avoidance of unnecessary intrusion
- Clear limits to confidentiality
- Protection from harm
- Researcher role boundaries

## Reflection

Safeguarding should not become a justification for routine over-collection of sensitive information. A proportionate protocol defines in advance what constitutes an escalation threshold and who is authorised to respond.
