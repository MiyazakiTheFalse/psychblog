# Ethical Decision-Making Case Matrix

> Simulated competency exercise demonstrating applied ethical judgement.

| Scenario | Initial decision | Key issue | Required response |
|---|---|---|---|
| Student recruited directly by lecturer who grades them | Modify | Power imbalance / coercion | Independent recruitment and clear separation from assessment |
| Distressed participant asks to skip a sensitive item | Proceed with safeguard | Autonomy | Allow skip with no penalty |
| Participant appears unable to understand what data will be collected | Do not enrol yet | Valid consent | Re-explain accessibly; enrol only if valid consent can be established |
| Researcher wants names in the analysis spreadsheet “for convenience” | Modify | Confidentiality/data minimisation | Use participant IDs; store identifiers separately |
| Immediate credible threat of serious harm disclosed | Safeguarding escalation | Limits of confidentiality | Follow pre-agreed minimum-necessary escalation pathway |
| Animal experiment duplicates an existing study without justification | Do not proceed as proposed | Replacement / unnecessary use | Review existing evidence and justify scientific necessity |
| Animal study proposes 100 subjects without sample-size rationale | Modify | Reduction / scientific validity | Provide defensible design and sample-size justification |
| Procedure causes avoidable distress when a lower-burden method exists | Modify | Refinement | Use the lower-burden validated method |

## Reflection

Ethics is not a binary checklist. A competent researcher must identify competing duties, distinguish inconvenience from genuine risk, document the reasoning behind decisions, and modify study design when safeguards can reduce harm without undermining the research question.
