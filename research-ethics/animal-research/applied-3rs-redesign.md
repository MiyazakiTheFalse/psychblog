# Applied 3Rs Ethical Redesign Exercise

> **Portfolio status:** Simulated ethical-review exercise. No animal research was conducted.

## Original hypothetical proposal

**Question:** Does chronic environmental stress impair learning and memory in laboratory mice?

**Original design:** Two groups of mice would experience either standard housing or repeated environmental stressors, followed by behavioural learning tasks.

## Ethical concern

A scientifically interesting question is not, by itself, sufficient justification for animal use. The researcher must ask whether the question can be answered without animals, whether the design uses no more animals than scientifically necessary, and whether procedures can be refined to minimise pain, suffering, distress and lasting harm.

## Replacement

Before animal work is considered:

- Search existing human and animal datasets for secondary analysis opportunities.
- Assess whether human observational research can address the psychological component.
- Examine computational or statistical models of stress-learning relationships.
- Consider in-vitro or other non-animal approaches where the biological mechanism permits.
- Conduct a systematic evidence review to avoid unnecessary replication.

**Decision:** Animal use should proceed only for questions that cannot reasonably be answered using suitable non-animal approaches.

## Reduction

If animal use remains necessary:

- Pre-register the primary hypothesis and outcomes.
- Conduct an appropriate prospective sample-size/power justification.
- Avoid unnecessary parallel groups.
- Use repeated measures where scientifically and ethically appropriate.
- Standardise procedures to reduce unexplained variance.
- Share appropriately reusable data to reduce avoidable duplication.

Reduction does not mean using an arbitrarily small sample. An underpowered study can waste animals without producing reliable knowledge.

## Refinement

Potential refinements include:

- Use the least distressing procedure compatible with the scientific question.
- Habituate animals to handling and testing environments.
- Use appropriate enrichment and social housing where compatible with welfare and design.
- Define frequency of welfare checks.
- Specify observable intervention criteria.
- Predetermine humane endpoints.
- Ensure trained personnel conduct procedures.
- Review whether behavioural tasks themselves can be shortened or modified.

## Harm-benefit assessment

**Potential scientific benefit:** Better understanding of mechanisms connecting chronic stress with learning and memory.

**Potential harms:** Distress from environmental manipulation, handling and behavioural testing.

**Ethical conclusion:** The study would require substantial redesign before approval. The research question should first be tested against Replacement options; any remaining animal protocol must be scientifically robust, adequately justified and refined to reduce welfare burden.
