# Animal Welfare Monitoring Plan

> Simulated portfolio exercise only.

## Objective

Demonstrate how welfare monitoring is built into study design rather than treated as an afterthought.

| Domain | Example observations | Response |
|---|---|---|
| Appearance | Coat condition, posture, visible injury | Increase monitoring; veterinary/research review |
| Behaviour | Reduced exploration, abnormal inactivity, aggression | Assess severity and potential intervention |
| Food/water | Reduced intake | Prompt welfare assessment |
| Body condition | Unexpected deterioration | Apply predefined intervention threshold |
| Procedure response | Persistent adverse response after handling/testing | Pause or modify procedure |
| Social behaviour | Isolation or conflict outside expected range | Review housing/refinement options |

## Monitoring schedule

Monitoring frequency should be proportionate to the expected severity of the procedure and increased during or after higher-risk periods.

## Humane endpoints

Endpoints must be defined before data collection. They are triggered by observable welfare criteria rather than waiting for severe deterioration.

Possible actions include:

1. remove the animal from the procedure;
2. provide appropriate supportive or veterinary care;
3. modify or discontinue the stressor;
4. humanely end participation according to the approved veterinary protocol.

## Record keeping

Each welfare intervention should record:

- date/time;
- animal identifier;
- observation;
- severity judgement;
- action taken;
- person responsible;
- follow-up outcome.

## Research-quality link

Welfare and scientific quality are connected. Uncontrolled distress can introduce unwanted variance and confounding, so refinement can improve both ethical acceptability and interpretability.
