# Ethics of Psychological Research Involving Animals

This is a competency demonstration only. No animal research was conducted.

## Ethical starting point

Animal research requires a scientific and ethical justification that cannot be reduced to whether a procedure is technically permitted. A proposed study should demonstrate that the research question is important, the method is necessary, alternatives have been considered and expected knowledge gain is proportionate to animal burden.

## The 3Rs

### Replacement
Use non-animal alternatives where they can answer the question, including existing datasets, human research, computational models, cell/tissue methods, simulation or less sentient systems where scientifically appropriate.

### Reduction
Use the minimum number of animals compatible with a valid design. Poorly powered research is not automatically more ethical: exposing animals to procedures without a realistic prospect of answering the research question can itself be wasteful.

### Refinement
Reduce pain, distress and lasting harm through housing, handling, habituation, enrichment, appropriate endpoints, anaesthesia/analgesia where relevant, skilled procedures and careful monitoring.

## Design quality is an ethical issue

An animal study should pre-specify:
- research question and hypothesis
- primary outcomes
- sample-size rationale
- randomisation where appropriate
- blinding where feasible
- inclusion/exclusion rules
- stopping and humane endpoints
- analysis plan

Weak design increases the risk that animal use produces uninterpretable evidence.

## Harm-benefit assessment

Potential scientific benefit should be weighed against:
- invasiveness
- duration
- pain or distress
- deprivation or restraint
- social disruption
- cumulative burden
- likelihood that findings will be informative and transferable

The existence of prior animal literature does not automatically justify another study.

## Governance

Any real project would require the relevant institutional review, legal permissions and trained supervision before work begins. A student portfolio or simulated protocol is not approval to conduct animal research.

## Reporting and transparency

A complete research culture should include accurate reporting of methods, exclusions and negative findings so that unnecessary replication and avoidable animal use are reduced.

## Evidence boundary

This document demonstrates understanding of ethical principles, the 3Rs and design-related ethical reasoning. It does not claim animal-handling competence, licensure, laboratory training or completed animal research.
