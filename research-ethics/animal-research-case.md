# Animal research ethics case analysis

## Fictional case
A researcher proposes an animal learning experiment to study behavioural flexibility following environmental change.

## Ethical review questions

### Replacement
Could the hypothesis be answered using existing datasets, human volunteers, computational modelling, organoids or other non-animal methods?

### Reduction
If animal use is scientifically necessary, has the design justified sample size and avoided unnecessary duplication while retaining adequate scientific validity?

### Refinement
Can handling, housing, procedures, monitoring or endpoints be modified to minimise pain, distress and lasting harm? Are humane endpoints explicit?

### Scientific validity
Poorly designed animal research is ethically problematic because animal burden cannot be justified by an uninterpretable study. Measures, exclusions and analysis should therefore be specified prospectively.

### Competence and governance
Researchers must have the relevant training, supervision and institutional/legal authorisation. Welfare monitoring and adverse-event procedures must be clear.

## Boundary
This document demonstrates conceptual familiarity with ethical reasoning and the 3Rs. It does not claim practical animal-handling competence or authorisation to conduct animal research.
