# Vulnerable participants ethics case analysis

## Fictional case
A study proposes interviews with 16 to 18-year-old students about social rejection, low mood and self-harm thoughts.

## Core ethical questions
- Is inclusion scientifically necessary, or could the question be answered using a lower-risk population?
- What makes participants vulnerable in this specific context?
- How will competence and voluntariness be supported rather than assumed?
- What are the limits of confidentiality if immediate risk is disclosed?
- Who has safeguarding responsibility and what escalation pathway is approved?
- Could school recruitment make students believe participation affects grades or staff perceptions?
- Are questions proportionate to the research objective?
- How are withdrawal, de-identification and data-retention limits explained?
- What support information is available during and after participation?
- How will researchers avoid diagnostic or stigmatising language?

## Decision principle
Vulnerability is not a blanket reason for exclusion. Inclusion requires a scientifically justified question, proportionate safeguards, transparent limits on confidentiality, appropriate consent/assent arrangements, data minimisation and formal ethical approval.

## Boundary
This is a fictional ethics exercise. It is not an approved protocol.
