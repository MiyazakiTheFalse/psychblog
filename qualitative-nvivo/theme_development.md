# Theme development audit trail

## Research question
How do undergraduate students describe what makes it easier or harder to begin and sustain work on important assignments?

## Stage 1: Familiarisation
The fictional excerpts were read repeatedly with attention to initiation, emotional meaning, environmental structure, competing responsibilities and accounts that resist the label of procrastination.

## Stage 2: Initial coding
Coding remained relatively close to the extracts. Examples include productive displacement, commitment anxiety, actionable feedback, caring constraints and immediate reward competition.

## Stage 3: Candidate themes
Five candidate themes were developed:

1. **Avoidance regulates immediate discomfort**
2. **Uncertainty makes starting feel unsafe**
3. **Evaluation threat shapes initiation**
4. **Structure changes the action environment**
5. **Delay is not always procrastination**

A sixth candidate, **Action changes internal state**, is retained provisionally because it is currently represented by only one especially clear extract (P12). It should not be promoted to a major theme merely because it fits my theoretical interests.

## Stage 4: Negative and complicating cases
P05, P08, P11 and P15 complicate a simple avoidance account. Their delays are described as financial prioritisation, deliberate incubation, caring constraints or extended quality work. These cases prevent the analysis from collapsing all late action into self-regulatory failure.

P12 complicates a purely emotion-first account by describing motivation as following behaviour.

## Stage 5: Reflexive review
My behavioural orientation creates a risk of privileging immediate reinforcement and environmental cues. The theme set was therefore reviewed specifically for social, material and evaluative explanations. The theme **Delay is not always procrastination** was retained because it challenges my preferred explanatory frame rather than supporting it.

## Stage 6: Naming and scope
Themes are intended to capture patterned meaning rather than keyword frequency. Counts may be useful for audit purposes but are not treated as prevalence estimates.

## NVivo mapping
In NVivo these materials could be represented as:
- 18 source excerpts
- participant cases P01 to P18
- initial-code nodes
- candidate-theme parent nodes
- reflexive memos
- a case-by-theme matrix for organisation only

The interpretive decisions remain the researcher's responsibility.
